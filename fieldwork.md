# Fieldwork — BH3 Mess, IIIT Allahabad

## Visit Details

- **Place:** BH3 Mess, Boys Hostel 3, IIIT Allahabad (IIIT Prayagraj), Jhalwa Campus
- **Mess contractor:** KMK Global Limited (they run both BH-2 veg and BH-3 non-veg messes)
- **Date:** 21 November 2026
- **Time spent:** About 25 minutes (6:15 PM — 6:40 PM, went during dinner service)
- **What I did:** Walked into the mess right when dinner started, took a photo of the packed hall, counted roughly how many seats were occupied and how many guys were in the queue. Then went near the kitchen area and talked to the head cook while he was supervising serving. Spotted the leftover container from lunch (still sitting near the kitchen exit) and took a photo of that too.

## Conversation with the Cook

- **Name:** Suresh Yadav 
- **Role:** Head cook at BH3 mess, working here for 6 years. Employee of KMK Global Limited.
- **Context:** Talked to him near the serving area right when dinner was starting. He was keeping an eye on the serving counter. I told him I'm doing a college project on food waste in the mess and wanted to understand how they decide quantities. He was chill about it, happy someone was asking.

### 3 things he said:

1. **"Hum weekly menu ke hisaab se saman lete hain, Monday se Sunday tak fix hai. Store se utna hi aata hai utna banana padta hai."** (We get materials based on the weekly menu, it's fixed Monday to Sunday. Whatever comes from the store, we have to cook all of it.)
   The procurement is weekly — KMK buys raw materials in bulk based on 200 students × 7 days. The cook can't send material back or adjust on the fly. Even on Friday night when he knows only 100 students will show up, the chicken and rice have already been procured and will spoil if not cooked. The waste is built into the procurement cycle.

2. **"Sabse zyada Friday raat ko bachta hai. Bacche Thursday-Friday se jaane lagte hain. Aur kaddu, soyabean, pattagobhi — ye toh koi khata hi nahi."** (Most waste is Friday dinner. Students start leaving Thursday-Friday. And kaddu, soyabean, pattagobhi — nobody eats these at all.)
   He's fully aware of both waste patterns — the Friday attendance crash and the unpopular items. He sees full containers come back every single time. But knowing it and being able to do anything about it are completely different things.

3. **"Jis din chicken ya chole hota hai us din sab aate hain. Baaki din 150-160 aate hain lekin tab bhi 200 ka khana banta hai."** (On chicken or chole days everyone comes. Other days 150-160 show up but we still cook for 200.)
   Attendance swings by 30-40% based purely on what's on the menu. Chicken curry Monday dinner, chole bhature Saturday lunch, chana masala Wednesday dinner — these are the days the mess is packed. But the cook prepares the same quantity regardless because that's what KMK's material allocation assumes.

### 1 thing that surprised me:

When I asked about sweets and desserts (gulab jamun on Monday, fruit custard Tuesday, kaju katli Friday, ice cream Saturday), he laughed and said **"meetha toh kabhi nahi bachta bhai, ek chamach bhi nahi. Kabhi kabhi toh ladke extra maangte hain aur hum mana karte hain."** (Sweets are never wasted, not even a spoonful. Sometimes students ask for more and we have to refuse them.)

This genuinely surprised me. Everything else — rice, dal, sabzi — wastes in varying amounts. But desserts have a 0% waste rate. Students who won't touch kaddu sabzi will eat every last gulab jamun. So the "unpopularity" problem is very item-specific. If the mess committee swapped one unpopular sabzi slot with a dessert-heavy day or a popular sabzi, waste would drop significantly without changing the budget much. The cook knows this but nobody's asked him.

## Raw Data — `data.csv`

### How I collected it:

Data covers 7 days: Sunday Nov 15 to Saturday Nov 21, 2026.

- **Nov 21 (Saturday):** Direct observation. I was physically present during dinner service, saw the leftover container from lunch, counted students for 10 minutes and estimated the total. Suresh bhaiya helped me estimate leftover quantities by looking at the containers — he's pretty accurate at eyeballing kg from fill levels, been doing it for 6 years.
- **Nov 15–20:** Got these numbers from Suresh bhaiya's memory. He remembers the past week fairly well because the menu repeats weekly so each day has a pattern he recognises. For prepared quantities he gave standard amounts ("rice toh 20-22 kilo hota hai roz"). For leftovers he estimated — "kitna bacha tha" — and was more precise about recent days (Thu, Fri) and rougher about Sun/Mon.
- **Student counts** are approximate throughout. For Nov 21 I counted. For other days, Suresh bhaiya gave rough figures ("Monday dinner mein 165-170 honge... Friday raat mein bus 100-110").
- I didn't get data for every single meal of every day. Some breakfasts are missing because Suresh bhaiya couldn't remember clearly — "breakfast mein toh kuch nahi dikhta, jo bhi ho jaldi khatam hota hai ya nahi hota."
- Used the menu card on the wall to verify which items were served on which day — cross-checked with Suresh bhaiya's recall.

Data is in [data.csv](./data.csv) — 45 entries across 7 days, item-level prep and leftover quantities for breakfast, lunch, and dinner.
