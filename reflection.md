# Reflection

Going in I thought this was a simple problem — the cook makes too much food, he should just make less, done. I figured I'd collect some data, run some averages, and have a neat little script. But sitting in the mess and actually talking to Suresh bhaiya completely shifted how I see it. The cook isn't the problem. He's arguably the most informed person in the whole chain — he knows exactly which days are dead, which items nobody touches, which meals everyone shows up for. The problem is structural. KMK procures materials on a weekly contract for 200 students. The mess committee sets the menu once per semester. The cook just executes whatever is written on that menu card and cooks whatever raw material shows up from the store. The person who understands demand the best has literally zero control over supply.

So my tool isn't really for the cook. It's a report card for the mess committee and KMK. It's evidence. "Look, you're wasting 140+ kg of food a week because you refuse to adjust for Fridays and you keep putting kaddu and soyabean on the menu." That reframing only happened after the fieldwork and I wish I'd understood it going in.

If I had to cut something, I'd cut the per-item breakdown table honestly. Everyone in BH3 already knows that kaddu and soyabean suck and chole and chicken are great. The actually useful output is the day-of-week attendance pattern and the specific kg recommendations — "make 13 kg rice on Friday dinner not 20." That's what you can print and tape to the kitchen wall. The rest is supporting evidence for people who want numbers.

AI helped me a lot with the pandas code — groupby aggregation syntax, formatting aligned columns in terminal output, structuring the recommendation logic. It also pointed me to the UNEP report which I wouldn't have found on my own. Where it completely wasted my time was suggesting I build a Flask web dashboard with charts. Spent like 30 minutes going down that path before I realised — the cook doesn't use a computer. A web dashboard is for my portfolio, not for solving his problem. Scrapped it. It also pushed me towards linear regression to "predict future waste" which is pointless with 7 days of data and would be a black box that nobody in the mess kitchen can understand or trust. Simple averages and patterns are what actually work here.
