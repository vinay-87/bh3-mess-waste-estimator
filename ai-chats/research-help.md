# AI Chat: Research Help

## Context
Used ChatGPT to help find studies on institutional food waste and understand existing solutions before going to the mess.

---

**Me:** I'm working on a project about food waste in my college hostel mess. Around 200 students in BH3 at IIIT Allahabad, mess is run by a contractor called KMK Global. They cook the same amount every day even when attendance varies a lot. Looking for research on this kind of institutional food waste problem.

**AI:** Institutional food waste is well-documented. The key sources you'd want to look at are:
1. UNEP Food Waste Index Report — most comprehensive global data on food service waste
2. Indian-specific studies on hostel/canteen food waste — a few published papers on ResearchGate
3. FSSAI's Save Food Share Food initiative materials

The main finding across all literature is that institutional kitchens in developing countries waste 20-40% of prepared food, primarily because they cook fixed quantities without tracking demand or adjusting for attendance.

**Me:** Found the UNEP report. The main insight relevant to us?

**AI:** The report says that just measuring and tracking waste — even crudely — reduces it by 15-25% without any other intervention. Making waste visible changes kitchen behavior. For most institutional kitchens in India, there's literally no tracking at all, so even a basic logging system would be a step up.

**Me:** What about existing apps or tech solutions? I found some food waste apps but they're all about redistributing surplus, not preventing it.

**AI:** Right. Apps like Too Good To Go and FSSAI's initiative connect surplus food with charities or discount buyers AFTER it's been cooked. They solve disposal, not prevention. Enterprise solutions like Winnow and Leanpath do prevention via smart scales and AI, but they cost thousands of dollars and need hardware. For a college mess run by a contractor where the cook doesn't use a smartphone, you need something way simpler — essentially a lookup table or printed sheet.

**Me:** Yeah the cook actually knows all the patterns but the mess committee and KMK procurement don't use his knowledge at all.

**AI:** That's a classic organizational disconnect — people closest to the problem have least decision-making power. Your tool could serve as a bridge: take the cook's implicit knowledge, back it with data, and present it to the mess committee in a format they can act on.
